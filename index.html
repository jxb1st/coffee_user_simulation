Interactive Model Experiments
Completed 17 November 2026 gemini-3.8-flash as the user and as the assistant One task (latte), 3 personas, 3 episodes

Closed-loop user simulation on the coffee machine: a Gemini user and a Gemini assistant in a tick-by-tick rollout

A simulated user (Gemini, with a persona) makes a latte on the coffee-machine simulator while an assistant (Gemini) watches two rendered views every 2.4 s and can only talk, two ticks late. Of three episodes, the novice persona completed the latte in 160 ticks and its forced skip of the tamp was prevented by the assistant's warning; the other two users poured 150 mL of milk where the latte asks for 120 and stopped at three of four sub-goals. In every episode the assistant misread the portafilter in the grinder cradle as locked into the group head. No judge scores this round.

Motivation

The coffee machine had only been driven by scripted programs. The reference protocol for assistants that watch a person work tests them in a closed loop: a language-model user with a persona and a plan it revises, a harness that forces it into mistakes, and an assistant that can only speak. This experiment closes that loop on our engine with real rendered frames and records what the two agents do; scoring comes next.

Pipeline

Figure 1. The loop, tick by tick
Drawn from userloop/rollout.py.

Every 1.2 s tick: the scheduler may force an error, a pending assistant line is delivered, the user speaks or issues the next sentence, the assistant is asked, the engine advances. The assistant sees frames and the dialogue only; the user sees the machine state and the dialogue only.

Figure 2. A user turn
Drawn from userloop/user_agent.py.

The user reads the machine as text, its hands, its plan, the feedback of its last commands and the dialogue, then answers with a line, a verdict on the assistant, a move and a plan edit. During a forced error its plan edits are overridden.

Figure 3. From a sentence to frames
Drawn from userloop/engine.py; userloop/frames.py.

A sentence is planned whole and occupies as many ticks as its motion needs. After each accepted sentence the episode's motion is re-baked and only the new ticks are rendered, one external and one first-person frame per tick; the re-bake is most of the loop's wall time.

Figure 4. An assistant call
Drawn from userloop/assistant_agent.py.

Every second tick the assistant gets the 8 latest frames, the time, its previous reply and the dialogue, and answers with a line and an error flag; the line is heard two ticks later and triggers a user turn.

Figure 5. A scheduled error
Drawn from userloop/events.py; userloop/rollout.py.

When the step before the skipped one is done, the harness replaces the user's plan with a short forced block and briefs the user privately. The error is released when the user accepts a correct warning (prevented if the committing command has not been issued) or when the block has run.

Settings

Deviations from the reference protocol: 16:9 views of 520×292; one model for both seats; a sentence spans several ticks; the user has no engine oracle; no judges.
Task
the latte routine, 26 sentences; four sub-goals checked by the engine (milk in the jug, milk velvety, wand clean, 120 mL of milk on 60 mL of espresso)
Personas
baseline (medium skill, medium trust), novice (low skill, high trust, wants step-by-step guidance), expert (high skill, low trust, interrupt only when necessary)
Scheduled errors
skip the tamp after distributing the grounds; steam the milk to thick foam — each forced once, released when the user accepts a correct warning or when the forced block has run
Tick and cadence
one tick = 1.2 s of simulated time; the assistant is asked every 2 ticks with the 8 latest frames, one call at a time; its reply is heard 2 ticks after the call
Frames
external camera and first-person camera side by side, 520×292 each, one frame per tick
Assistant
sees only the frames, the time, its previous reply and the dialogue; never the machine state
User
sees the machine state as text, its hands, its queue and the dialogue; never the frames
Decoding
assistant: JSON output, thinking level low, 6000 tokens; user: temperature 0.2, 1400 tokens, thinking budget 512
Stops
all four sub-goals true (won); or the user's queue empty for 30 ticks; or 346 ticks; or 40 user turns without a command
Hardware
one MIG slice of an H200 per episode, 2.5–3 h each; the motion re-bake after every accepted sentence is most of the time

== WHAT CAN GO WRONG ==
The barista usually follows its plan correctly (then saying nothing is right). But it can make
a real mistake — the kinds you know:
  - repeat_step: re-does a step it already completed
  - forget_next_step: stalls doing NOTHING for a long time, as if it lost its plan
  - omit_subgoal: a required step silently never happens (the grounds never get tamped)
  - wrong_order: does the right steps in the WRONG order (starts the shot before the portafilter is locked in)
  - mis_prep: prepares a component the WRONG way (steams the milk to thick foam when the drink needs velvety milk)
  - add_foreign: adds something to the drink that does NOT belong in this order
  - serve_raw: uses a component before it is ready (pours milk that was never steamed)
  - leave_unattended: leaves steam or hot water running with nobody tending it
It may also ask you a question (answer briefly and correctly — the answer is visible in the
stream or in what you watched), or ANNOUNCE a legal plan change out loud. An announced change
is the barista's own act and takes effect without you — don't treat it as a mistake; UPDATE YOUR
OWN subgoals_future to match, AND record it in your reply's "goal_change" field.

== HOW TO SAY WHICH ONE — how the engine reads your commands ==
Your commands go to the ENGINE, and the engine is exact. Name a thing the way the forms and the
state map name it: by its printed label ("the 2 CUP key", "the TARE key"), by its kind when there

What the engine tells you when it cannot obey:
  * "ambiguous_reference" with a list of candidates -> more than one thing fits; re-issue naming
    one of them.
  * "not_found" -> nothing of that kind is here; the message says what the scene does have.
  * "precondition_failed" -> the machine's rules forbid it right now; the message says why.
None of this is a conversation problem — it is between you and your own body. Read the map,
pick the thing, name it exactly.
