Interactive Model Experiments
Completed 17 November 2026 Gemini 3.8 Flash as the user and as the assistant One task (latte), 3 personas, 3 episodes

Closed-loop user simulation on the coffee machine: a Gemini user and a Gemini assistant in a tick-by-tick rollout

A tick is 1.2 s of simulated time. The assistant looks at the 8 latest frames every 2 ticks; what it says reaches the user 2 ticks (2.4 s) later. Both the user and the assistant are Gemini 3.8 Flash.

A simulated user makes a latte on our coffee-machine simulator while an assistant watches two camera views and can only talk. Three runs, one per user persona. The novice user finished the latte, and the assistant's warning stopped it from skipping the tamp. The other two users poured 150 mL of milk where the recipe wants 120 and never finished. In all three runs the assistant mistook the portafilter in the grinder for one locked into the machine.

Motivation

We want an assistant that watches someone work and speaks up before a mistake. Testing it with real people is slow, so a second model plays the user: it has a personality, a plan, and it is secretly made to slip up now and then. This page shows that whole loop running on our coffee machine with rendered video, and what the two sides said to each other.

Pipeline

Figure 1 is the whole loop; the others open on click and show one part each.
Figure 1. The loop, tick by tick

Every 1.2 s tick: the scheduler may force an error, a pending assistant line is delivered, the user speaks or issues the next sentence, the assistant is asked, the simulator advances. The assistant sees frames and the dialogue only; the user sees the machine state and the dialogue only.

Figure 2. A user turn

The user reads the machine as text, its hands, its plan, the feedback of its last commands and the dialogue, then answers with a line, a verdict on the assistant, a move and a plan edit. During a forced error its plan edits are overridden.

Figure 3. From a sentence to frames

A sentence is planned whole and occupies as many ticks as its motion needs. After each accepted sentence the episode's motion is re-baked and only the new ticks are rendered, one external and one first-person frame per tick; the re-bake is most of the loop's wall time.

Figure 4. An assistant call

Every second tick the assistant gets the 8 latest frames, the time, its previous reply and the dialogue, and answers with a line and an error flag; the line is heard two ticks later and triggers a user turn.

Figure 5. A scheduled error

When the step before the skipped one is done, the harness replaces the user's plan with a short forced block and briefs the user privately. The error is released when the user accepts a correct warning (prevented if the committing command has not been issued) or when the block has run.

Settings

A persona sets how skilled the user is, how much it trusts the assistant and how much guidance it wants.
Task
make a latte; the user starts from the recipe (26 steps) and may revise it; four sub-goals: 1) milk in the jug, 2) milk velvety, 3) steam wand clean, 4) 120 mL of milk on a double shot
Personas
1) baseline — average skill, average trust; 2) novice — little skill, trusts the assistant, wants step-by-step help; 3) expert — skilled, distrustful, wants to be left alone
Planned mistakes
1) skipping the tamp; 2) over-steaming the milk. Each is forced on the user once, and undone if the user accepts a correct warning in time
Timing
one tick is 1.2 s; the assistant looks every 2 ticks and is heard 2 ticks later
Views
1) an external camera; 2) the user's first-person view — side by side
The user sees
1) the one-line order (make a latte in the glass cup: velvety milk on a double shot) and the 12 rules of how this coffee machine behaves; 2) its persona card and the recipe as its starting plan; 3) the list of command sentences it can use (the action space below) and the four sub-goal names; 4) a text state map of the coffee bar: every container and what it holds, every control, part and place; its own hands, its plan and what it has done so far; 5) the assistant's sentences only. It sees no video
The assistant sees
1) the same order and the same 12 rules; 2) the user's three questionnaire answers (speed, alerting, interruption) and nothing else about it, not its skill or trust; 3) the same command sentences and the four sub-goal names; 4) every 2 ticks the 8 latest frames, the conversation so far and its own previous notes; 5) the kinds of mistakes a user can make. It does not see the recipe, the state map, the user's plan or which mistakes are planned
A run ends
1) when the latte is made; or 2) when the user has had nothing planned for 30 ticks

Action space

This is the user's whole vocabulary. A sentence of any other shape is refused with a reason, and the user has to rephrase. The clips are the device simulation's one short program per action. Full definitions on the device simulation site.
The sentences the user can say

Each action can be said in a few ways. Angle brackets are blanks to fill from the state map; a part in parentheses with a question mark is optional. Under each clip: the camera, the sentences in the clip, what they did. The last line on the right is what the engine requires and what it changes.

  • Look at

    • look at <thing>

    <thing> anything listed: a key, knob, part, place or object

    Turns the head and eyes to the thing named. Nothing else changes.

  • Read

    • read <ref>
    • check <ref>
    • what does <ref> read

    <ref> a named thing of the scene: a container, a part, a control or a place

    Changes nothing. The reply reports what the thing shows: the scale in grams, the pressure gauge, the lights (power, CLEAN, DESCALE, filter), how full the water tank, hopper, drip tray or knock box is, what the jug holds (milk, foam, temperature), what a cup holds (contents, crema, whether it is warm).

  • Wait

    • wait until <cond> for at most <dur>
    • wait until <cond>
    • wait <dur>
    • wait for <dur>

    <cond> something the machine can bring about: <container> holds <qty> of <substance> · the shot stops · the grinder stops · the cycle ends · the scale reads <qty> · the milk is microfoam | velvety | thick foam · the milk reaches <°C> · the CLEAN | DESCALE light goes off · the <key> key has run <qty>

    <dur> a duration: 3 seconds, 1.5 s, 2 min

    Lets time pass. Whatever is running keeps running: a shot, the grinder, hot water, steam, a cleaning cycle. “Wait until” stops the moment the condition comes true; it is refused at once if nothing running could bring it about, and it gives up after 60 seconds unless a shorter limit is given.

  • Press

    • press <key>
    • press <key> <N> times
    • hold <key> for <seconds> s

    <key> POWER, FILTER SIZE, PROGRAM, 1 CUP, 2 CUP, TARE

    <N> a count: twice, three times, 5 times

    <seconds> a hold time from 0.05 to 10 s: a 1 CUP / 2 CUP key held 1 s or longer runs for the hold; 10 s with the cleaning disc locked in starts a cleaning cycle, 5 s on 2 CUP with descaler in the tank descales

    Pushes a key. The machine reacts only if the key is pressed far enough.

    • turn <knob or dial> by <angle> clockwise | counter-clockwise

    <knob or dial> grind amount dial, grind size dial, steam dial

    <setting> grind amount dial: a dose in grams from 8 to 22; grind size dial: a grind size 1 to 16, or fine, medium, coarse; steam dial: hot water, off or steam

    <angle> a quarter turn, half a turn, 90 degrees, 45°, two clicks; a setting outside the control's range is refused

    Turns a knob or dial to a setting, or by an angle.

    <part> steam wand, drip tray, water tank
