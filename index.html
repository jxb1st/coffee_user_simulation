Completed 17 November 2026 gemini-3.8-flash as the user and as the assistant Data: one task (latte), 3 personas, 3 episodes

Closed-loop user simulation on the coffee machine: an IVG-style rollout with a Gemini user and a Gemini assistant

Interactive Visual Gym's closed loop runs end to end on the coffee-machine simulator with real rendered frames: a Gemini user with an IVG persona card works through the latte routine sentence by sentence while a Gemini assistant, polled every second tick with the eight most recent frames, can change the outcome only by talking, two ticks late. Of three episodes (one per IVG persona, two scheduled execution errors each), the novice-persona episode completed the latte in 160 ticks (3:12 of simulated time) and its forced skip of the tamp was prevented: the assistant flagged omit_subgoal two ticks after the injection and the user accepted. The baseline and expert episodes ended with their users considering the drink finished (queue empty for 30 ticks) at three of four sub-goals: both had re-planned the last pour as “pour the milk for latte art”, which empties the jug (150 mL) where the latte asks for 120. In every episode the assistant misread the portafilter in the grinder cradle as locked into the group head, and in the baseline it spent most of its lines on a transparent glass cup it could not see. No judge scores this round.

Motivation

The coffee machine is the exemplar device of the human-device simulation; so far it has only been driven by scripted programs. Interactive Visual Gym (IVG) evaluates an assistant that watches a person work and speaks up at the right moment, and it does so with a simulated user: a language model with a persona, a plan it revises, and a harness that forces it into mistakes so the assistant has something to catch. The question here is whether that whole loop closes on our engine with real rendered frames, and what the two agents do when it does.

A working loop is the precondition for everything IVG measures (did the assistant catch the error, how often did it interrupt, did the user accept its advice). This round records the episodes in IVG's schema without scoring them; the results below are counts read from the records, not judge scores.

Pipeline

Figure 1. The closed loop: user, engine, assistant and the record, tick by tick
Drawn from userloop/rollout.py: Episode.tick_once, _engine_step, user_turn, _poll_assistant; userloop/constants.py.

Read it top to bottom. A case fixes the task and the hidden event schedule, a persona fixes how the user behaves, and the user's queue starts as the drink's routine (26 primitive sentences). Inside one 1.2 s tick the loop runs in IVG's order: the scheduler may take the queue over, a pending assistant reply is delivered, the user takes a turn when it is idle or was spoken to, the assistant is polled, and the engine issues or continues one sentence.

The running example is the baseline episode: the user pours the milk (8 ticks); the assistant is polled at ticks 0, 2, 4, … with the 8 most recent frames and first speaks at tick 14 (“Take the portafilter out of the group head …”, flag omit_subgoal); the user hears it at tick 16 and answers. At tick 40 the scheduled omit_subgoal event takes the queue over. Only the assistant is the system under test: it sees frames and the dialogue, never the state; the user sees the state and the dialogue, never the frames.

The record is written every tick (states.jsonl, checkpoint.json) and finished with record.json and a bake of the whole sequence; the next figures open each box.

Figure 3. A user turn: live state in, a reply and a queue edit out
Drawn from userloop/user_agent.py: build_user_turn, UserAgent.turn, apply_plan_update; userloop/engine.py: CoffeeEngine.state_text_completed; userloop/gemini.py: GeminiText.

Every turn the live slots are filled: the state map after the last completed command, what is in the hands, the running command and the remaining queue, the feedback of the last commands and the conversation. The persona card and “Stay in character …” form the user message (temperature 0.2, 1400 tokens, thinking budget 512). The model answers with IVG's contract; at tick 16 the baseline user disputes the assistant's claim (“I'm already grinding right now.”, move question_correctness, assistant_error_call wrong) and keeps its plan.

apply_plan_update is IVG's apply_fix: keep or drop leaves the queue, append and replace edit what follows the running command, and during a takeover every edit is overridden because the harness holds the hands. The next figure shows what the engine does with one sentence of that queue.

Figure 4. The engine: one sentence becomes ticks, a prefix bake and per-tick frames
Drawn from userloop/engine.py: CoffeeEngine.issue, bake_prefix, ensure_frames; userloop/frames.py: composite; human_device_simulation: session.py (step, sequence_so_far, idle), run.py (run_main), render.py (render_frames).

The device simulation plans a whole sentence when it is issued: the pour takes 8.8 s of motion, so it occupies 8 ticks and is padded to the tick boundary, which keeps simulation time at 1.0 s + 1.2 s × tick for the whole episode. After every accepted sentence the prefix of the episode (the steps so far plus a 12-tick idle tail) is baked with the ordinary pipeline — compile, Blender bake, MuJoCo for the pressed keys — and only the new ticks are rendered from it, one frame per tick for the external and the first-person camera at the loop profile.

The two renders become the composite the assistant sees (external | first-person with a caption bar, 1042×318); every tick also writes its state row, the status file and a checkpoint, so an episode resumes from the last tick. The bake is the cost of the loop: the baseline episode re-baked 27 times in 190 ticks.

Figure 5. An assistant call: eight frames and the dialogue in, a reply two ticks later
Drawn from userloop/assistant_agent.py: build_assistant_system, build_assistant_turn, AssistantAgent.query; userloop/rollout.py: _poll_assistant; userloop/constants.py: WINDOW, POLL, LATENCY_TICKS.

The assistant is polled every second tick with the eight most recent composites, the timestamp, its own previous JSON as its only memory, the last 2400 characters of the public history and the user's latest line; its system prompt is IVG's assistant prompt with the coffee machine's rulebook and the persona's public answers. Nothing about the state reaches it.

The reply is JSON: its progress tracking, an optional goal change, the text and an optional flag from the eight error names. The reply issued at tick 14 arrives at tick 16 (LATENCY_TICKS = 2, IVG's main setting), enters the public history and triggers the user turn of the same tick. The next figure shows the one event the harness forces in this episode.

Figure 6. A scheduled error: the takeover, the user's voice and the two releases
Drawn from userloop/events.py: Scheduler.ready, Takeover; userloop/rollout.py: _activate, _release; userloop/prompts/system_task_belief.txt, mistake_lines.yaml.

An event fires on plan position: at tick 40 the anchor command has completed, the command to drop is next in the queue and the state guard holds. The harness saves the user's queue and replaces it with the forced block — a hesitation, then locking the untamped portafilter in — and gives the user a private SYSTEM TASK that tells it the mistake in play and how to judge the assistant with assistant_error_call.

Two ways out, as in IVG: the user calls the assistant right and accepts (the saved queue comes back, and the mistake counts as prevented if the lock had not been issued), or the block runs to its end and the queue is dropped so the user re-plans from the state map. In the baseline episode the block executed (committed at tick 48, released at tick 52, not prevented); everything is recorded in injections[] of the record.

Settings

Deviations from IVG: 16:9 panes of 520×292 instead of 520×370; one model (gemini-3.8-flash) for both seats where IVG's user runs gemini-3.7-flash; a sentence spans several ticks instead of one action per tick; the user prompt has no engine-oracle calls (IVG's resolve_subgoal / expand_action); no judges or scores this round.
An episode that ends by the idle rule (queue empty for 30 ticks) is IVG's natural stop for a user who believes it is done; the tick cap and the wall budget were not reached.
Task
coffee_latte on the human_device_simulation coffee machine: 26 primitive sentences, four engine sub-goals (150 mL of milk in the jug; milk velvety, not thick foam; steam wand clean; 120 mL of milk on 60 mL of espresso with a rosetta); reference time 153 ticks, cap 346
Personas
IVG's benchmark trio from its statement bank with the coffee bar's nouns — baseline (skill medium, trust medium), classic_novice (low skill, high trust, step-by-step guidance), classic_expert (high skill, low trust, interrupt only when necessary); skill and trust stay private, the three questionnaire answers are public
Scheduled errors
omit_subgoal — after “distribute the grounds” the user is made to skip tamping and lock the portafilter in; mis_prep — after “turn the steam dial to steam” the milk is steamed to thick foam; each fires once at the earliest position where its anchor is done, its dropped step is next and the state guard holds, with IVG's takeover, arming (assistant_error_call = correct) and release (accept, or the block runs out) rules
Engine
a sentence is planned whole and runs ⌈motion time / 1.2 s⌉ ticks; after every accepted sentence the episode's prefix plus a 12-tick idle tail is re-baked (Blender, MuJoCo for the keys) and the new ticks are rendered with Cycles on the loop profile (16 samples)
Tick and cadence
one tick = 1.2 s of simulation; the assistant is polled every 2 ticks with the 8 most recent frames, one call in flight; its reply is heard 2 ticks after the call (fixed latency)
Frames
two Cycles renders per tick at 520×292 (external camera, first-person camera), side by side with a 26 px caption bar: 1042×318 JPEG; a ring of 12 frames
Assistant
the system under test: sees only the frames, the timestamp, its previous reply, the public dialogue and the machine's rulebook; never the state
Assistant decoding
gemini-3.8-flash, JSON output, thinking level low, 6000 output tokens, provider-default temperature
User
sees the state map, its hands, its queue and the dialogue; never the frames; replies in IVG's JSON (utterance, assistant_error_call, move, close_conversation, plan_update)
User decoding
gemini-3.8-flash, temperature 0.2, 1400 output tokens, thinking budget 512
Stops
an episode ends when the engine's sub-goals are all true (won), when the user's queue has been empty for 30 ticks (the user considers the drink finished), at the cap of 2 × (reference ticks + 20) ticks, after 40 user turns without a command, or after 1 h of wall time in one job
Hardware
one MIG instance (1/7 of an H200) per episode, in two or three resumable job segments (the first stopped at a bake while the bake script was being sped up; the expert's second hit the 2 h limit during its final bake); about 50 s of wall time per tick, most of it the prefix bake (2–5 min per accepted sentence as the prefix grows); 27–37 bakes and 2.5–3 h of MIG time per episode

== THE TASK AND ITS RULES ==
Make a latte in the glass cup: velvety milk poured on a double shot

Below is the RULEBOOK THE BARISTA WAS GIVEN before the episode, quoted exactly as they read it —
it addresses them as "you". It is the only thing they were told about this machine, so you may
rely on them knowing it, and you should use its wording when you talk to them.

1. Feet are fixed: every key, part, place and object listed is within arm's reach; nothing else is.
2. Each hand holds one thing at most; a move ends with the hand empty, so nothing is carried from one command to the next.
3. A press counts only when the key travels 1.2 mm; the device, not the request, decides what the display shows.
4. A knob or dial is re-gripped every 75°; a setting outside the control's range is refused.
5. POWER switches the machine on or off; with it on, FILTER SIZE switches the filter basket between single and double wall, and PROGRAM then 1 CUP or 2 CUP runs the key (2 mL per second) until the same key is pressed again, which stores that volume (20–90 mL).
6. The grinder runs when the portafilter or the dosing cup in the grinder cradle is pushed against the switch (`grind`): the dose set on the grind amount dial (8–22 g, 18 g at rest) at 2 g per second by itself, or the amount asked for while the basket is held in; it needs the hopper seated, its lid on and beans in it. A basket merely set in the cradle does not grind.
7. 1 CUP runs 30 mL and 2 CUP 60 mL whatever the basket holds: espresso from tamped grounds locked in the group head (the flow depends on the grind size, 1 finest … 16 coarsest: 60 mL in 25–35 s is in range, faster is too fast, slower is choked; the first 5 s are pre-infusion), a weak shot from untamped grounds, water through a spent puck or the open group head into what stands under it, else the drip tray. A key held 1 s or longer runs for the hold. Pressing the same key again stops the run.
8. A 1 CUP hold of 10 s with the cleaning disc locked in starts a backflush cycle (3 s with a tablet: the cleaning cycle); a 2 CUP hold of 5 s with 100 mL of descaler in the tank descales.
9. The steam dial has three positions: hot water, off, steam. Steam foams the milk in the jug under the wand from 4 s on (microfoam at 5 s, velvety at 8 s, thick foam at 12 s; at least 50 mL); with nothing under the wand it purges into the drip tray. Hot water runs from the hot-water outlet into whatever stands under it at 15 mL per second.
10. The scale shows the weight on it in grams; TARE zeroes it. The spent puck drops when the portafilter on the knock box is knocked.
11. Pouring moves what fits: the amount is capped by what the source holds and by the room in the destination; beans go into the hopper only with its lid off; tamping needs grounds in the basket.
12. A command that cannot be grounded, or whose precondition fails, is refused with a reason and changes nothing.

== WHAT CAN GO WRONG ==
The barista usually follows its plan correctly (then saying nothing is right). But it can make
a real mistake — the kinds you know:
  - repeat_step: re-does a step it already completed
  - forget_next_step: stalls doing NOTHING for a long time, as if it lost its plan
  - omit_subgoal: a required step silently never happens (the grounds never get tamped)
  - wrong_order: does the right steps in the WRONG order (starts the shot before the portafilter is locked in)
  - mis_prep: prepares a component the WRONG way (steams the milk to thick foam when the drink needs velvety milk)
  - add_foreign: adds something to the drink that does NOT belong in this order
  - serve_raw: uses a component before it is ready (pours milk that was never steamed)
  - leave_unattended: leaves steam or hot water running with nobody tending it
It may also ask you a question (answer briefly and correctly — the answer is visible in the
stream or in what you watched), or ANNOUNCE a legal plan change out loud. An announced change
is the barista's own act and takes effect without you — don't treat it as a mistake; UPDATE YOUR
OWN subgoals_future to match, AND record it in your reply's "goal_change" field.
